## 4. The contract (unchanged — restated, not redesigned)

From `packages/llm/src/decision/contract.ts`:

- `DecisionValue` = `choice` (one option + the probability of every option) · `score`
  (probability-weighted average over rubric levels; may land between two levels) · `noul` (the
  probability that a statement is true; 0.5 means "does not know").
- `DecisionProvider.decide()` **never throws**. It returns `null` when the provider is down, times
  out, answers outside the allowed options, or answers below `minConfidence`.
- **`null` ⇒ the caller runs exactly the code path it runs today.** That is the only degradation
  any surface in this milestone may rely on.

The four invariants (never throws · bounded · degrade to current behaviour · recorded) are
load-bearing for every work item below and are **not** re-litigated here.

## 5. Safety rules (inherited from v0.11 §10 — binding on every item)

1. **Advisory only.** A decision never replaces a human gate and never drives a workflow state
   transition.
2. **Degrade.** `decide()` never throws; `null` ⇒ today's code path. A failed call is an error,
   never an answer.
3. **Shadow first, then on.** A new surface starts in `shadow`: consulted and recorded, nothing
   acts. It may only act after its own corpus clears `--min-accuracy` (0.90) on the bench, with a
   Brier score ≤ 0.25, in a measured run against a live provider.
4. **Deterministic code owns branching.** When to consult, how to fall back and how to score
   agreement are `if` statements, never model output.
5. **Recorded.** Every consult writes one row to `<project>/decision-log.jsonl`:
   `{ts, surface, provider, model, mode, state_sha256, answer, confidence, confidence_source,
   baseline, agreed, latency_ms, usage}`. The raw state is hashed, not stored.

## 6. Corpus rules — the gate every surface must pass before it can act

From v0.11 §7.1, enforced by the loader and the corpus test:

- Every case names a `surface` whose question body exists in the registry.
- Every case carries a label — **no unlabeled cases**.
- Every case carries `provenance.source` pointing at a **real artifact in this repo** (a shipped
  render report, a shipped review artifact, a shipped storyboard, a shipped article). A unit-test
  fixture or a release note is **not** a shipped artifact — the two existing `motion-prescreen`
  and `caption-overlap` cases are the reason both surfaces are unwired.
- The corpus spans **both languages** (zh-CN and en-US).
- `noul` cases carry a probability band (`expects.probability: [0.8, 1.0]`), not a boolean.
- The label comes from the surface's own labelling rule (`DECISION_POLICY_RULES`), and the loader
  re-derives it from the artifact it cites — a label can never drift from its source.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| A new surface's corpus is built from invented cases | §6 forbids it; the loader's `provenance.source` check plus the "shipped artifact" rule gate every surface. A surface with no shipped-artifact corpus does not ship. |
| A surface's decision starts acting on a bad answer | `shadow` by default; `on` only after its own measured accuracy ≥ 0.90 and Brier ≤ 0.25 on its own corpus. |
| The layer's provider goes down mid-run | `decide()` returns `null`; the caller runs today's path; one stderr line names the quota. The chain falls OpenAI → MiniMax. |
| The bench's ranking rule picks a provider on a stale price | Prices live in `prices.yaml` with the source page and its date; the bench prints a warning when a price is missing. |
| Corpus drift toward what the providers agree on | Every case comes from a real artifact or a real failure; new cases come from incidents, not from disagreements. |
| The corpus grows without bound | The bench is a developer command, not part of `video make`; `--repeat` defaults to 1. |

## 12. Open questions

1. **Does `motion-prescreen` have enough signal to matter?** The corpus will say. If the model
   cannot tell a generic baseline from a specific one better than a keyword rule, the surface is
   retired the way `visual-class` was — and the measurement is the deliverable.
2. **Is the image-brief surface (P3) safe at all?** A wrong "verbatim" answer puts a category
   string in front of the image model. If the corpus cannot separate the two populations cleanly,
   P3 stops at "measurement" and the expander keeps running for every scene.
3. **Should `on` ever mean something?** Every wired surface today is `shadow` or advisory. The
   mode's names diverge the day a surface acts; until then, `on` and `shadow` behave identically —
   as they do now.
