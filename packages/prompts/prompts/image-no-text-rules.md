HARD RULE — the image carries NO READABLE writing.
- Never REQUEST text, titles, captions, subtitles, headings, labels, signs, banners, logos, watermarks, or typography. Asked-for words come back as confident gibberish.
- The test is whether a VIEWER could read it. Legible words, a readable sign, a real title, a UI, a watermark that says something — those are the failures. Marks the viewer cannot make out — the texture of a printed page at a distance, blurred impressions, a page of dense illegible strokes, a small seal or signature whose characters cannot be made out — are NOT a failure. They read as a page, a sign, an artist's mark, and that is what the scene wanted. Acceptance: if the audience cannot tell the marks are wrong or odd characters, it passes.
- Written content that must be legible is composited AFTER generation by the renderer, never asked for here.
- If the scene genuinely needs printed material (a page, poster, shopfront, screen, noticeboard), describe it as printed but unreadable: "a page covered in dense illegible strokes", "blurred marks on a sign", "an unreadable document". Ask for the SHAPE of the material — a page, a notice, a signboard — and let the marks stay illegible. Never ask for the words.
- Do not ask for an empty gallery print, a signed plate, or "an artist's work" — that framing invites a mark with legible characters. State it positively instead: an unsigned, unmarked illustration, or printed material whose characters cannot be made out.
- RESERVE THE OVERLAY ZONE — captions, titles and component graphics are composited over this image AFTER generation. Ask for the empty space explicitly: "keep the lower third quiet and uncluttered", "no objects intruding into the bottom band". Merely avoiding clutter is not enough; state the empty zone as a requirement, because captions need somewhere to land.
- Treat every exclusion as an instruction, not a guarantee. The model still sometimes produces confident gibberish. Anything that must be legible gets drawn by the renderer, never by the image model.
